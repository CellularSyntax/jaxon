\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have presented jaxon, a fully differentiable peripheral nerve
fiber simulator that reproduces the canonical MRG, Sundt, Sweeney
and Rattay axon models with $100\,\%$ of $1\,152$ threshold-test
configurations under the $1\,\%$ error criterion vs.\ NEURON,
achieves a $\sim 30\times$ throughput improvement over \texttt{pyfibers}
at $N=200$ fibers (rising to $69\times$ at $N=1\,000$ and $122\times$
at $N=10\,000$ Sweeney) on a single A16 GPU, and exposes
end-to-end gradients with respect to per-contact stimulus amplitudes
and arbitrary waveforms.  The third capability is the conceptual
contribution: previous PNS optimisation pipelines have been
gradient-free, restricting selectivity search to grid sweeps or
evolutionary methods that scale poorly with the dimensionality of
the design space.  With jaxon, rectangular-pulse amplitude optimisation
achieves \textbf{median $\mathrm{SI}=1.000$ on every supported fibre
model} (Section~\ref{sec:results-selectivity},
Table~\ref{tab:selectivity}): across 125 randomised Hussain-style
nerves spanning MRG (at both 5.7\,$\mu$m and 10\,$\mu$m), Sweeney,
Sundt and Rattay, cross-model mean SI is $0.955$, $79\,\%$ of seeds
reach $\mathrm{SI}\ge 0.95$, and $70\,\%$ reach the theoretical
maximum $\mathrm{SI}=1.0$.  The fibre-model generalisation matters:
the same optimisation pipeline applies to myelinated and unmyelinated
fibres with no algorithmic change --- only the per-model amplitude
scaling of Table~\ref{tab:opt-hparams} differs, reflecting the
two-order-of-magnitude span of single-contact activation thresholds.
The same forward kernel supports autodiff over arbitrary per-contact
waveforms, giving access to optimisation regimes (kHz block,
charge-balanced chirps, anodic-leading patterns) that have so far
been studied piecemeal in the literature.

\subsection{Comparison with prior work}

The closest direct comparator is pyfibers~\cite{hussain2024}, which
established the convention of wrapping NEURON axon models from Python
and using random-search / FD-gradient optimisation for selectivity
design.  jaxon's contribution relative to pyfibers is twofold:
(i) the JAX implementation removes the Python-NEURON IPC overhead
that limits pyfibers throughput and makes vmap-batched parallel-fiber
forward passes possible on a single GPU; and (ii) the JAX implementation
admits end-to-end gradient computation, which pyfibers does not.
The validation results in
Section~\ref{sec:results-validation} confirm that this re-derivation
matches NEURON's accuracy on the canonical benchmark suite, so the
throughput and gradient gains do not come at the cost of model
fidelity.

The wider differentiable-simulation context for jaxon spans both
the JAX-ecosystem frameworks
\texttt{jaxley}~\cite{deistler2024} and
\texttt{JAX MD}~\cite{schoenholz2020jaxmd}, and the broader
autodiff-through-ODE literature initiated by the Neural ODE
work~\cite{chen2018neuralode}.  \texttt{jaxley} provides the
underlying multi-compartment HH machinery used by jaxon's solver.  \texttt{jaxley}
ships canonical CNS Hodgkin--Huxley channels and a generic cable
solver but does not include the calibrated peripheral nerve fiber
models that the PNS modelling literature relies on; jaxon's contribution
within this ecosystem is the peripheral specialisation: the coupled
$(V_i, V_{\mathrm{px}})$ MRG solver, the four unmyelinated fiber
models, the validation-against-NEURON harness, and the
selectivity-optimisation pipeline are domain-specific and not provided
out-of-the-box by \texttt{jaxley}.

GPU-accelerated multi-compartment neuron simulation has a separate
prior literature outside the autodiff context.
\texttt{NeuroGPU}~\cite{benshalom2022neurogpu} ports NEURON
biophysical models to CUDA and reports up to $200\times$ speedup over
NEURON-MPI on a single GPU, and
\texttt{Brian2GeNN}~\cite{stimberg2020brian2genn} accelerates Brian
spiking-network simulations via the GeNN code generator with
comparable throughput gains.  These pipelines accelerate the forward
pass but, like pyfibers, expose no end-to-end gradients;
gradient-based optimisation over design variables still requires the
finite-difference detour the present work is designed to obviate.

\subsection{Why the mixed-diameter case is hard}

A natural extension of single-diameter spatial selectivity is to
exploit \emph{type selectivity}: different axon diameters have
different chronaxies, so in principle a sufficiently short-duration
pulse should preferentially recruit large fibers and a sufficiently
long-duration pulse should preferentially recruit small ones.  In
practice the chronaxie ratio between 5.7 and 14\,$\mu\mathrm{m}$ MRG
fibers is small (under 2$\times$) at the stimulus durations used in
clinical PNS~\cite{wongsarnpigoon2010energy}, and the all-fibers-fire
state---which simultaneously satisfies the target-firing constraint
and violates the off-target-silence constraint---sits at a globally
attractive minimum of the WQ loss whenever amplitudes are
unconstrained.  We attempted to break the attractor by (i) softening
the activation proxy with a per-node sigmoid of temperature
$\tau=0.1$ to widen the gradient-informative window at saturation,
and (ii) regularising the amplitude energy
($\lambda \sum I_k^2 / K$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:wq-loss}) so that the optimiser
must find a low-amplitude selective solution.  Neither modification
sufficed: with $\lambda$ small enough not to dominate, the
all-fibers-fire equilibrium remained attractive; with $\lambda$ large
enough to escape it, the optimiser converged to the trivial all-amps-
zero solution.

We interpret this negative result as an honest delimitation of where
the current cuff geometry and loss formulation suffice and where they
do not, rather than as a limitation of jaxon itself.  In particular,
the underlying differentiable forward solver is gradient-informative
in all regimes; the failure to converge is a loss-landscape property,
not a gradient-availability one.  Two plausible avenues to crack
this regime are: (i) replacing the homogeneous bath
$V_e$ with an anatomically realistic FEM-derived $V_e$ that breaks the
symmetry between target and off-target fascicles~\cite{musselman2023};
and (ii) reformulating the objective as a constrained optimisation
(minimise $\sum I_k^2$ subject to all-target-firing as a hard
constraint, using a primal-dual or augmented-Lagrangian method).
We leave these as the natural follow-up directions.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Single-cuff six-contact geometry.}
All selectivity results in this paper use a fixed cuff radius
($1500\,\mu\mathrm{m}$), fixed number of contacts ($K=6$), and a
single-cuff configuration.  Larger contact counts, conformable cuffs
and multi-cuff arrays are increasingly common in clinical practice
but were out of scope for the present validation paper.

\paragraph{Homogeneous bath $V_e$.}
We computed $V_e$ from an analytic point-source formula in homogeneous
bath conductivity, which is the convention used by pyfibers but is
not anatomically accurate.  The natural extension is to import
$V_e$ from a finite-element solve over a segmented nerve geometry;
jaxon's interface accepts $V_e$ as a pre-computed
$[K, n_{\mathrm{fiber}}, n_{\mathrm{comp}}]$ tensor and so is agnostic
to its origin.  We are presently integrating jaxon with our in-house
voxel-grid FEM pipeline and expect to report on selectivity in
anatomically realistic nerves in a follow-up paper.

\paragraph{No biological variability in fiber parameters.}
All fibers in a given diameter share identical channel densities,
geometric ratios and resting state.  Inter-fiber variability is known
to broaden activation distributions and lower achievable
selectivity~\cite{aristovich2021}.  Future work will sample from
diameter-stratified parameter distributions estimated from
electrophysiological recordings.

\paragraph{Activation proxy versus full spike detection.}
The activation proxy
\begin{equation*}
  a_f = \min\!\bigl(\max_t \mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{Na}}(t)\big|_{\text{first node}},\
                    \max_t \mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{Na}}(t)\big|_{\text{last node}}\bigr)
\end{equation*}
correctly classifies recruitment when an action potential
propagates to both ends.  It is approximate in pathological regimes
(failed propagation, partial recruitment in dendrites with branching
or blocked nodes).  None of these regimes was encountered in the
straight-axon MRG simulations of this paper, but they would warrant
revisiting the proxy for branched-fiber or blocked-conduction
applications such as DC block or kHz conduction
block~\cite{kilgore2014block}.

\subsection{Outlook}

The combination of GPU throughput and end-to-end gradients positions
jaxon for several natural next-step applications:
(i) selectivity optimisation on anatomically realistic FEM-derived
$V_e$ fields, which break the homogeneous-bath symmetry that limits
the current pipeline in the mixed-diameter regime and enable
fascicle-specific cuff design on patient-specific anatomies;
(ii) population-averaged designs that marginalise over realistic
nerve-to-nerve variability via a single batched forward pass;
(iii) inverse modelling of recorded extracellular field data to
infer fiber recruitment from cuff-recorded compound action
potentials~\cite{vissamsetti2025cap}; and
(iv) closed-loop adaptive stimulation policies trained by
backpropagation through the forward model.  Each of these would
require a gradient-free pipeline to perform thousands or millions
of redundant forward simulations; with jaxon, they reduce to standard
gradient-based optimisation.
