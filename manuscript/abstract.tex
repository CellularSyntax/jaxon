Computational models guide the design of selective peripheral nerve
stimulation, but to remain tractable they almost universally reduce
each fascicle to one or a few representative fibers.  Whether this
reduction biases the selectivity it predicts has been untestable,
because testing it requires simulating entire fiber populations under
optimized extracellular fields.  We built \emph{jaxon}, a fully
differentiable, GPU-native reimplementation in JAX of canonical
peripheral nerve fiber models --- the myelinated
McIntyre--Richardson--Grill and Sweeney axons and the unmyelinated
Sundt and Rattay C-fibers --- validated against
\texttt{pyfibers}-wrapped NEURON (\textbf{$99.6\,\%$ of 943
configurations within $1\,\%$ threshold error}) and reaching a
\textbf{geometric-mean $\sim\!820\times$ speedup} (over five
model/parameterization variants) at $N = 100{,}000$ fibers (vs.\
8-thread CPU \texttt{pyfibers}; $\sim\!200$--$300\times$ at the
${\sim}1000$-fiber scale used here) on one NVIDIA A100, fast enough to
optimize and re-score whole-nerve populations.  Using FEM-derived fields over a 12-contact
cuff on 29 histology-segmented vagus nerves (18 swine, 11 human),
we use it to show that a standard reduced-order shortcut ---
optimizing on one representative fiber per fascicle --- overestimates
how much selectivity is actually deliverable to the full myelinated
fiber population --- as opposed to the small unmyelinated C-fibers,
whose selectivity limit is a separate, larger effect left to companion
work.  The
overestimate is negligible in swine (median ${\sim}0.01$) but substantial
and heavy-tailed in human (median $0.14$, up to $0.45$ on the
largest-fascicle nerves; Mann--Whitney $p = 0.01$), because human
fascicles are large; it appears as a failure to recruit the target
fascicle, not off-target spill.  Sampling more fibers helps but does
not close it on the biggest fascicles.  Re-scoring the candidate on the
full population --- cheap in jaxon --- recovers most of it (human
residual ${\sim}0.02$), so the actionable gap in reduced-order pipelines
is population-scale validation, now feasible, rather than a different
optimizer.  jaxon is open-source and slots into existing pipelines as
a gradient-enabled replacement for the NEURON forward solver.
