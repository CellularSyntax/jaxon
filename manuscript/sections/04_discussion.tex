\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Using population-scale differentiable simulation, we have shown that
the reduced-order fiber sampling standard in GPU-accelerated
selectivity modeling --- reducing each fascicle to one or a few
representative fibers and reading selectivity off that reduced set ---
overestimates the selectivity actually delivered to the nerve when used
that way.  The error is not a fixed offset: this \emph{deployment
penalty} is governed by fascicular architecture --- the number of fibers
per target fascicle --- and is therefore small in swine (median $0.009$)
but larger in human (median $0.083$), a between-species translational
bias that reduced-order models cannot detect because they never
simulate the fibers the bias acts on.  Mechanistically the penalty is
a failure to recruit the large human target fascicle from a field
tuned to a few sampled fibers, not off-target spill or reduced drive.
It is also largely \emph{recoverable}: because the full-population forward
pass is cheap in jaxon, re-scoring or selecting a sparse-optimized design
on the population removes most of the penalty (residual human
${\sim}0.02$), so the actionable message is that population-scale
validation --- not a different optimizer --- is what reduced-order
pipelines lack.
This was measurable only because jaxon simulates the full
${\sim}1000$-fiber population: a GPU-native, fully differentiable
reimplementation of the canonical MRG, Sweeney, Sundt and Rattay axon
models that reproduces NEURON ($99.6\,\%$ of $943$ configurations under
the $1\,\%$ criterion) at a geometric-mean $\sim\!820\times$ throughput
gain (peak $1\,377\times$) on a single A100.  Throughput is the
enabling capability here: prior NEURON pipelines are slow enough that
full-population optimization is impractical, forcing the per-fascicle
centroid reduction, whereas jaxon's fast forward pass makes
gradient-based optimization (finite-difference over the contacts)
tractable directly on the full population --- and re-scoring on that
population is what exposes the penalty
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-duke}--\ref{sec:results-sparse}).  The
exact end-to-end gradients jaxon also exposes --- which NEURON-wrapped
pipelines cannot --- enable the high-dimensional waveform design left
to future work.

\subsection{Comparison with prior work}

The closest comparator is pyfibers~\cite{hussain2024}, which wraps
NEURON axon models in Python with random-search / FD-gradient
optimization.  jaxon's contribution is twofold: it removes the
Python--NEURON overhead, making vmap-batched GPU forward passes
possible, and it admits end-to-end gradients that pyfibers lacks ---
without loss of fidelity (Section~\ref{sec:results-validation}).  The
wider open-source PNS-modeling ecosystem --- ASCENT, NRV~\cite{couppey2024nrv}
and PyPNS~\cite{lubba2019pypns} --- shares the same gradient-free,
CPU-NEURON basis, so a differentiable, GPU-native forward solver is, to
our knowledge, new to peripheral nerve modeling.
jaxon is complementary to this ecosystem rather than a replacement for
it: the subject-specific finite-element fields used here are produced by
our companion open platform, golgi~\cite{haberbusch2026golgi}, which
builds the anatomy, meshes and solves the extracellular field, and
characterizes fascicular and fiber-type selectivity with threshold-based
NEURON and surrogate backends.  jaxon contributes the differentiable,
GPU-native fiber solver and the population-scale sampling-penalty analysis
that those threshold pipelines do not perform, and drops in as an
additional backend.
Within the JAX ecosystem, \texttt{jaxley}~\cite{deistler2024} supplies
the multi-compartment Hodgkin--Huxley machinery but ships only
canonical CNS channels; jaxon adds the peripheral specialization --- the
coupled $(V_i, V_{\mathrm{px}})$ MRG solver, the calibrated Sweeney,
Sundt and Rattay models, the NEURON-validation harness and the
selectivity pipeline.

Other GPU neuron simulators ---
\texttt{NeuroGPU}~\cite{benshalom2022neurogpu},
\texttt{Brian2GeNN}~\cite{stimberg2020brian2genn},
\texttt{CoreNEURON}~\cite{kumbhar2019coreneuron} and \texttt{Arbor} ---
accelerate the forward pass but none models \emph{extracellular} field
stimulation, the regime relevant to cuff electrodes, and none exposes
end-to-end gradients.  CPU NEURON has thus remained the only
established simulator of extracellular peripheral-fiber stimulation,
which is why we benchmark against it and why jaxon's GPU-native,
differentiable extracellular forward pass is itself a capability gap
that the existing tools do not fill.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of the deployment-penalty finding.}
The penalty is established \emph{in silico}, with one cuff and one FE
pipeline, on 29 nerves (11 human).  We therefore frame fascicular
architecture as the \emph{driver} the between-species penalty difference
scales with (cohort Spearman $r = +0.47$; the within-species trend is
directionally consistent but under-powered), not as a manipulated cause.
A diameter spot-check confirms the species ordering persists at $5.7$,
$7.3$ and $10\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ (human penalty $0.083$, $0.066$ and $0.054$
vs swine ${\sim}0.01$ throughout), so the direction is not an artifact of
the single diameter; we still expect the magnitude --- not the direction
--- to depend on fiber-type mix, cuff geometry, the conductivity model,
and on within-fascicle heterogeneity in fiber diameter, which by making a
per-fascicle centroid less representative can only widen the penalty.
Anchoring the effect experimentally is the natural next step; jaxon's
throughput makes the required population-scale sweeps tractable.

\paragraph{Single-cuff geometry.}
The histology selectivity results use a single 12-contact MultiContact
cuff (three axial rows $\times$ four angular columns) at a fixed
radius.  Larger contact counts, conformable cuffs and multi-cuff
arrays are increasingly common in clinical practice but were out of
scope for the present paper; jaxon's per-contact amplitude
parameterization extends to arbitrary contact layouts with no change
to the solver.

\paragraph{Simplified cuff finite-element model.}
The per-contact $V_e$ lead-fields for the histology cohort are computed
with a simplified cylindrical-cuff finite-element model rather than a
fully device-faithful encapsulation/electrode-impedance solve.  jaxon's
interface accepts $V_e$ as a pre-computed
$[K, n_{\mathrm{fiber}}, n_{\mathrm{comp}}]$ tensor and so is agnostic
to its origin, so a higher-fidelity FEM can be substituted without
touching the optimization pipeline.

\paragraph{Single fiber type, no biological variability.}
The selectivity results use one myelinated fiber type at a single
diameter, and all fibers of that diameter share identical channel
densities, geometric ratios and resting state.  This isolates the
sampling effect we set out to quantify, but it is an optimistic setting
for selectivity: with identical fibers a per-fascicle centroid is
maximally representative, so the deployment penalty we report is a
conservative lower bound, and inter-fiber variability is known to
broaden activation distributions and lower achievable
selectivity~\cite{aristovich2021}.  A distinct and, on the vagus, larger
limit on deliverable selectivity comes from the realistic \emph{mix} of
fiber types: our companion open platform~\cite{haberbusch2026golgi},
which supplies the finite-element fields used here, shows on these same
nerves that moving from a single-diameter idealization to a morphometry-based
A/B/C population collapses the selectivity of the small, high-threshold
cardiac fibers, whose recruitment forces currents that also engage
off-target fibers.  That fiber-type axis is orthogonal to the sampling
axis studied here and compounds it; jaxon's throughput makes the
population-averaged, multi-type sweeps needed to characterize their
interaction tractable, which we leave to future work.

\paragraph{Activation proxy versus full spike detection.}
Recruitment is scored by a sigmoid-smoothed activation proxy
(Section~\ref{sec:methods-opt}) whose binary limit is
\begin{equation*}
  a_f = \min\!\bigl(\max_t \mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{Na}}(t)\big|_{\text{first node}},\
                    \max_t \mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{Na}}(t)\big|_{\text{last node}}\bigr).
\end{equation*}
It correctly classifies recruitment when an action potential
propagates to both ends.  It is approximate in pathological regimes
(failed propagation, partial recruitment in dendrites with branching
or blocked nodes).  None of these regimes was encountered in the
straight-axon MRG simulations of this paper, but they would warrant
revisiting the proxy for branched-fiber or blocked-conduction
applications such as DC block or kHz conduction
block~\cite{kilgore2014block}.

\subsection{Outlook}

GPU throughput and end-to-end gradients position jaxon for several
next steps that are intractable for gradient-free pipelines:
fiber-type (diameter-based) selectivity via waveform-shape gradients;
population-averaged designs that marginalize over nerve-to-nerve
variability in a single batched pass; inverse modeling of cuff-recorded
compound action potentials to infer
recruitment~\cite{vissamsetti2025cap,eiber2021visceral};
and closed-loop stimulation policies trained by backpropagation through
the forward model.
