\section{Fibre-model equations and coupled solver}
\label{sec:app-equations}

This appendix gives the governing equations of the four implemented
fibre models and the coupled backward-Euler solver, summarised in
Section~\ref{sec:methods-models}.

\paragraph{MRG double-cable (myelinated).}
The McIntyre--Richardson--Grill model~\cite{mcintyre2002} represents
each myelinated axon as nodes of Ranvier (carrying the active Na$^+$,
slow K$^+$, persistent Na$^+$ and leak conductances) alternating with
ten passive internodal compartments (MYSA, FLUT and three STIN per
side), arranged as a double cable with intracellular ($V_i$) and
periaxonal ($V_{\mathrm{px}}$) potentials coupled through the
periaxonal resistance and myelin shunt.  Fibre diameter $D$ is the
single shape parameter; geometric quantities follow McIntyre's
diameter-dependent fits.  With
$\mathbf{V}_i, \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{px}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\mathrm{comp}}}$,
\begin{equation}
  C_m \frac{d\mathbf{V}_i}{dt} = \mathbf{A}_{ii} \mathbf{V}_i +
  \mathbf{A}_{ip} \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{px}} - \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ion}}(\mathbf{V}_i, \mathbf{m}) -
  \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{stim}}(t),
  \label{eq:cable-vi}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  0 = \mathbf{A}_{pi} \mathbf{V}_i + \mathbf{A}_{pp} \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{px}} + V_e(t,\mathbf{x}),
  \label{eq:cable-vpx}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A}_{**}$ are tridiagonal coupling matrices from the
per-compartment axial resistances, $\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ion}}$ collects
the four active currents (nodes only), and $V_e$ is the imposed
extracellular field.

\paragraph{Sundt (unmyelinated, single-cable).}
The Sundt C-fibre~\cite{sundt2015} is a uniform unmyelinated cable with
a transient HH-style sodium current $I_{\mathrm{Na}}$ (NaV1.7/1.8
calibration via voltage shifts $\Delta_m = -6$\,mV, $\Delta_h = +6$\,mV),
a Borg--Graham delayed-rectifier $I_{\mathrm{Kdr}}$ and leak $I_L$:
\begin{equation}
  C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ax}}\mathbf{V} - I_{\mathrm{Na}}
  - I_{\mathrm{Kdr}} - I_L - I_{\mathrm{stim}}.
  \label{eq:cable-sundt}
\end{equation}
$V_{\mathrm{rest}} = -60$\,mV, $C_m = 1\,\mu$F\,cm$^{-2}$,
$\rho_a = 100\,\Omega\,$cm; densities and kinetics in
Table~\ref{tab:app-sundt}.

\paragraph{Sweeney (myelinated, single-cable).}
The Sweeney motor axon~\cite{sweeney1987} is a single-cable nodal
equation with the internode a passive sleeve, nodal current a fast
$m^2 h$ sodium plus leak~\cite{musselman2023}:
\begin{equation}
  C_m^{\mathrm{node}} \frac{dV}{dt} = \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ax}}\mathbf{V}
  - g_{\mathrm{Na}}\, m^2 h\, (V - E_{\mathrm{Na}}) - g_L\,(V - E_L)
  - I_{\mathrm{stim}}.
  \label{eq:cable-sweeney}
\end{equation}
Calibrated for $T = 37\,^\circ$C ($d_i = 0.6\,d$),
$V_{\mathrm{rest}} = -80$\,mV,
$C_m^{\mathrm{node}} = 2.5\,\mu$F\,cm$^{-2}$,
$\rho_a = 54.7\,\Omega\,$cm; constants in Table~\ref{tab:app-sweeney}.

\paragraph{Rattay (unmyelinated, generic HH).}
The Rattay model~\cite{rattay1989analysis} is a textbook HH baseline
for unmyelinated fibres with $g_{\mathrm{Na}} m^3 h$,
$g_K n^4$ and leak:
\begin{equation}
  C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ax}}\mathbf{V}
  - g_{\mathrm{Na}}\, m^3 h\, (V - E_{\mathrm{Na}})
  - g_K\, n^4\, (V - E_K) - g_L\,(V - E_L) - I_{\mathrm{stim}}.
  \label{eq:cable-rattay}
\end{equation}
Squid-axon rate constants temperature-corrected via
$Q_{10} = 2.247^{(T - 6.3)/10}$ at $T = 37\,^\circ$C;
$V_{\mathrm{rest}} = -70$\,mV, $C_m = 1\,\mu$F\,cm$^{-2}$,
$\rho_a = 100\,\Omega\,$cm; constants in Table~\ref{tab:app-rattay}.

\paragraph{Coupled backward-Euler solver.}
The MRG double cable is the only non-trivial solver case.  Because the
periaxonal equation~(\ref{eq:cable-vpx}) has no capacitive term it is
algebraic, so $\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{px}}$ is eliminated by
back-substitution before stepping $\mathbf{V}_i$.  At step $n$,
\begin{equation}
  \left(C_m / \Delta t \cdot I - \mathbf{A}_{ii}^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)\mathbf{V}_i^{(n+1)} =
  C_m / \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{V}_i^{(n)} - \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ion}}^{(n)}(\mathbf{V}_i, \mathbf{m})
  - \mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{stim}}^{(n)} - \mathbf{b}_e^{(n)},
  \label{eq:beuler-vi}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{A}_{ii}^{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathbf{A}_{ii} -
\mathbf{A}_{ip} \mathbf{A}_{pp}^{-1} \mathbf{A}_{pi}$ the Schur
complement of the periaxonal cable and $\mathbf{b}_e^{(n)} =
- \mathbf{A}_{ip} \mathbf{A}_{pp}^{-1} \mathbf{V}_e^{(n)}$ the
extracellular forcing.  Eq.~(\ref{eq:beuler-vi}) is solved by a
JAX-compatible Thomas tridiagonal sweep; gating variables advance
semi-implicitly.  The forward pass is wrapped in \texttt{jax.lax.scan}
over time steps, returning either the full voltage trace or only the
per-compartment maximum of $\mathbf{m}_{\mathrm{Na}}$ (the activation
proxy) at $\ge\!2$ orders of magnitude lower memory cost.

\paragraph{Per-sample FE model.}
For each sample, an automated pipeline (Golgi~\cite{haberbusch2026golgi})
converts the segmented cross-section into a watertight 3-D multi-domain
nerve-and-cuff FE model and computes the per-contact extracellular
lead field.  Briefly: (i) the segmentation is cleaned morphologically
and Ramer--Douglas--Peucker-simplified, then area-preserving
circularised to remove segmentation-specific shape bias while
conserving fascicle areas; (ii) the cross-section is extruded axially
and embedded in a concentric cuff (12 platinum contacts in 3 axial
rows $\times$ 4 angular columns, on a $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ silicone wall,
$5.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ long), a thin saline film ($150\,\mu\mathrm{m}$
clearance) and a muscle bath; (iii) the assembly is tetrahedralised
with Gmsh/OpenCASCADE and Netgen, refining to
${\approx}100\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ near the cuff and coarsening to
${\approx}1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ in the bulk; (iv) the electro-quasistatic
problem $\nabla \cdot (\sigma \nabla V) = 0$ is solved in
FEniCSx/DOLFINx with $P_1$ elements, anisotropic endoneurium
($\sigma_{\perp} = 1/6.0$, $\sigma_{\parallel} = 1/1.75$\,S/m) and
muscle ($0.086$ transverse, $0.35$ longitudinal\,S/m), isotropic
epineurium/encapsulation ($1/6.3$\,S/m), saline ($1.76$\,S/m),
silicone ($10^{-12}$\,S/m) and platinum contacts
($9.43 \times 10^{6}$\,S/m)~\cite{pelot2020perineurium,grill1994stimulus,gielen1984incorporation,horch2017neuroprosthetics};
(v) the perineurium is a zero-thickness contact-impedance sheet with
sheet resistance $R_s = d_{\mathrm{peri}} / \sigma_{\mathrm{peri}}$
($\sigma_{\mathrm{peri}} = 1/1149$\,S/m~\cite{pelot2020perineurium}),
$d_{\mathrm{peri}}$ taken per fascicle from the diameter-dependent
relation~\cite{pelot2020perineurium} (swine) or the anti-claudin-1
masks (human).  Because the problem is linear, the block matrix is
LU-factorised once with MUMPS and one $1\,\mathrm{A}$ solve per contact
yields the lead field $V_e^{(k)}$ in V/A for each of the 12 contacts.
