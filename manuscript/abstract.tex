Peripheral nerve stimulation is a rapidly expanding therapeutic
modality, but optimising electrode geometry and stimulus parameters
for fascicle-selective activation remains computationally expensive:
state-of-the-art pipelines wrap the NEURON simulator and require
thousands of forward simulations per design candidate.  We present
\emph{jaxon}, a fully differentiable, GPU-native reimplementation in
JAX of the canonical peripheral nerve fiber models, with no surrogate
approximation: the myelinated McIntyre--Richardson--Grill (MRG) and
Sweeney axons and the unmyelinated Sundt and Rattay C-fibers.  Built
on the \texttt{jaxley} differentiable Hodgkin--Huxley framework, jaxon
adds a batched coupled-cable backward-Euler integrator that simulates
large fiber populations --- up to $10^{5}$ axons --- fully in parallel
on a single GPU and exposes end-to-end gradients with respect to
per-contact stimulus amplitudes.  Validated against
\texttt{pyfibers}-wrapped NEURON across diameters
$0.3$--$16\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, eight stimulus waveforms and pulse widths
$0.02$--$1\,\mathrm{ms}$, \textbf{$99.6\,\%$ of 943 test
configurations agree within $1\%$ relative threshold error} and
conduction velocity matches to within $0.24\,\%$.  On a single NVIDIA
A100, jaxon reaches a \textbf{geometric-mean speedup of
$\sim\!820\times$ at $N = 100{,}000$ fibers} (up to $1{,}377\times$),
crossing parity with NEURON at $N \approx 10$.  As a demonstration of
the differentiability advantage, we optimise spatially selective
fascicular stimulation on a cross-species cohort of microCT-segmented
vagus nerves from the SPARC consortium, over a 12-contact cuff with
FEM-derived extracellular fields: gradient-based optimisation recovers
selective configurations where the cuff has spatial purchase and
honestly reports the geometric ceiling where it cannot.  jaxon is
open-source and slots into existing peripheral nerve stimulation
pipelines as a drop-in, gradient-enabled replacement for the NEURON
forward solver.
